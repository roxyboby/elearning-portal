ITIL 4 Foundation: Requirements

Analogy: Getting a Library Card

Think of the ITIL 4 Foundation exam like getting a library card. The library doesn't ask if you've read Shakespeare or have a PhD in literature. They just need you to show up, learn the rules (how to check out books, due dates), and pass their simple "test" (filling out the form correctly). The goal is to give you access to the library's resources, just as the ITIL Foundation gives you access to the language of modern IT service management.

The Key Requirements

  • 1. Training (Recommended)

    While not mandatory, taking an accredited training course (like this one!) is highly recommended. It ensures you cover all the necessary topics in a structured way.

  • 2. The Exam

    This is the only formal step. You must pass the ITIL 4 Foundation exam.

    • Format: 40 multiple-choice questions.
    • Duration: 60 minutes (75 minutes if English is not your first language).
    • Passing Score: 65% (you need to answer 26 out of 40 questions correctly).
    • Rules: It is a closed-book exam.

1.1: Service, Value, and Value Co-creation

Analogy: Ordering a Pizza

Imagine you order a pizza online.
  • The Service isn't just the pizza itself. It's the entire experience: the easy-to-use website, the payment processing, the delivery to your door, and the customer support if something goes wrong. You get the outcome (a delicious meal) without owning a pizza oven or hiring a delivery driver.
  • The Value is your personal satisfaction. Was it worth the price? Did it arrive hot and on time? Did it satisfy your hunger? This is subjective and defined by you, the customer. Your friend might order the same pizza and find less value if it arrives late.
  • Value Co-creation happens because the pizza place can't create value alone. You, the consumer, must participate. You provide a correct address, have money to pay, and are home to receive the pizza. Both you and the provider work together to create the final value.

Core Concepts

  • Service: A means of enabling value co-creation by facilitating outcomes that customers want to achieve. The customer does not need to manage the specific costs and risks associated with the service.
  • Value: The perceived benefits, usefulness, and importance of something. It is always defined by the service consumer (the recipient).
  • Value Co-creation: The mutual creation of value by the service provider and the service consumer. Value is not delivered; it is co-created.
  • Service Relationship: The cooperation between a service provider and a service consumer. This includes service provision, service consumption, and service relationship management.

1.2: Stakeholders in Service Management

Analogy: A School Play

Imagine a school putting on a play. Several people are involved in making it a success:
  • The Sponsor is the School Principal. They provide the budget and give the final approval for the play to happen. They authorize the cost.
  • The Customer is the Drama Teacher. They decide which play to perform, what the costumes should look like, and define what a "successful" performance is. They define the requirements.
  • The Users are the students—the actors, the stage crew, the musicians. They are the ones actively using the stage, the props, and the costumes to perform the play.
  • The Service Provider is the Drama Club itself, which organizes the auditions, rehearsals, and coordinates everything to deliver the final performance.

Core Concepts

  • Service Consumer: A generic role that can be broken down into three more specific roles: Customer, User, and Sponsor.
  • Customer: The role that defines the requirements for a service and takes responsibility for the outcomes of service consumption.
  • User: The role that uses services.
  • Sponsor: The role that authorizes the budget for service consumption.
